PREDICTIVE MODELING

How long might an active event persist?

A survival curve represents the probability that an already-active recorded event remains ongoing as time passes. It estimates persistence conditional on confirmation and elapsed event time—not when a disaster will start or whether a worksite is safe.

Kaplan–Meier survival analysis

An empirical baseline uses durations of historical events to estimate conditional persistence. It remains selected when a more complex candidate does not improve the validation result.

Discrete-time logistic hazard modeling

A fitted statistical model estimates recorded-event ending in successive time intervals. Those estimates produce a conditional persistence curve; they do not measure local exposure or injury risk.

RESEARCH EVIDENCE & LIMITATIONS

Test against history. Keep the limits visible.

  • Flood fitting uses 2021–2023 records for the displayed 2024 validation comparison. A separate 2025 period is evaluated after fitting through 2024. Related reporting episodes stay grouped, and episodes crossing fit/selection cutoffs are purged.
  • Calibration/reliability checks and episode-bootstrap intervals accompany prediction and duration errors. The 2025 Flood period was previously inspected; it is not a fresh untouched holdout. Retrospective evaluation does not establish operational safety validation.
  • Historical records describe regional reporting processes, not verified worker-site exposure. NHC best tracks are post-analysis, and mapped EIA locations do not establish active field work.
  • Missing or stale inputs remain unknown, never zero risk. Unsupported forecasts are withheld instead of substituting a different hazard's model.
  • The Monte Carlo chart checks model-implied sampling agreement. It is an archived experiment, not a live forecast, new historical backtest or proof of real-world accuracy.

Prediction is not permission.

Research scope and sources

The figures describe retrospective research and representative infrastructure locations. Models estimate persistence for certain already-active events. Their outlooks support human review; deterministic rules control alerts and the separate 0–10 hazard index.

  • 526,824 mixed records and measurements: 349,030 NOAA Storm Events records, 122,270 hourly station measurements and 55,524 NHC historical track points. These are source inventories, not independent training examples. Data coverage · NHC archive counts.
  • Six research duration targets: Flood, Flash Flood, High Wind, Heavy Rain, Winter Storm and Heat Index Exceedance. Heat-index research is restricted to the evaluated stations. Retrospective evaluations do not establish operational safety certification. Target scope.
  • 5,000 Monte Carlo draws: one seeded, offline flood-model distribution-consistency experiment. Historical backtesting evaluates predictive skill; sampling agreement alone does not. Evaluation and limitations.
  • 50 mapped reference locations: representative EIA infrastructure sites, with no implication of active crews or complete live coverage at each location. Location provenance.
